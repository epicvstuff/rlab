3D Molecular Dynamics Simulation

Simulating the Ideal Gas from First Principles  |  Year-End R Lab Project

Python 3 NumPy Matplotlib N = 100 particles L = 10 nm box Argon-like gas

Objective

Build a 3D Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulation of gas particles from first principles. By tracking the microscopic motion of individual particles, measure the macroscopic quantity Pressure and verify the Ideal Gas Law computationally.

P · V = N · kB · T

The simulation uses a constant-volume, constant-N cubic box with variable temperature. Particles undergo perfectly elastic wall collisions with no inter-particle interactions and no gravity.

PART I Velocity Initialisation

Each particle's velocity is drawn from the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution appropriate for a target temperature T. Three correction steps ensure physical accuracy:

  • 1 Random Sampling — each velocity component (vx, vy, vz) drawn from a normal distribution with σ = √(kBT / m)
  • 2 Zero Net Momentum — subtract the mean velocity from all particles so the gas has no bulk drift
    vi ← vi − (1/N) Σ vj
  • 3 Temperature Rescaling — scale all velocities so kinetic energy matches T exactly
    λ = √(Ttarget / Tcurrent)     vi ← λ · vi

Temperature is recovered from velocities via kinetic theory:

(3/2) kB T  =  (1/2) m ⟨v²⟩

PART II Microscopic Engine

Each time step dt = 10−13 s advances all N particles simultaneously using NumPy vectorisation.

Kinematics

rnew = rold + v · dt

Elastic Wall Collisions

When a particle crosses a wall (pos < 0 or pos > L) the perpendicular velocity component is reversed and the particle is reflected back inside the box. Each collision imparts an impulse to the wall:

Δp = |2 · m · v⊥|

All impulses are accumulated each step to compute pressure over the measurement window. Inter-particle collisions and gravity are ignored (ideal gas approximation).

PART III Pressure Calculation

After 500 equilibration steps, the simulation runs for 3 000 production steps. All momentum transfers to the six walls are summed, then divided by total surface area and elapsed time:

P = ΣΔp  /  (6L² · Δt)

For 100 argon-like particles at 300 K in a 10 nm box this yields P ≈ 4.1 × 105 Pa (≈ 4 atm), consistent with the ideal gas law prediction.

PART IV Deriving the Ideal Gas Law

The simulation was run at 10 different temperatures (100 K – 1000 K) with N and V held constant. Pressure scales linearly with temperature, confirming P ∝ T.
